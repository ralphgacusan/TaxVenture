# Choices, Public Trust and endings

Part of the [approved campaign outline](README.md). Narrative conditions are agreed; exact new numerical rewards, timing, final support mapping and interactions remain unfinished.

## 1. Distinct meanings

| System | Meaning | Story boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mastery/XP/first-check result | Learning/assessment performance | First-attempt errors do not automatically determine community trust or the ending. |
| Public Trust hearts | Earned public confidence in Elijah's commitments and service | Knowing choices, explanation and follow-through matter; popularity does not make unsupported claims true. |
| Coins/upgrades | Office-budget progression and optional equipment | Buying a decoration or finding a chest is not an agreed moral achievement. Purchases cannot be required to win. |
| Supported findings | What records actually establish | Correctness can be repaired; final claims need evidence independent of trust. |

Use the existing Public Trust direction, not old accuracy-score reputation totals as a substitute. Current docs use five proportional hearts and a numeric value out of 100. Exact UI implementation is outside this outline.

## 2. General choice philosophy

Offer believable immediate benefits from harmful decisions: reassurance, speed, processing convenience or assistance. Do not label options cartoonishly good and evil. The player must understand the professional concern before a knowing commitment is recorded.

Delayed consequences reveal impact, not a hidden rule invented afterward. A shortcut can briefly look successful and later undermine trust when residents learn what occurred. Responsible behavior earns lasting support through completed action, not simply selecting a polite line.

Ordinary wrong fields, draft calculations, incorrect explanations, review retries, a justified hold or a missed office cutoff remain learning/time matters. Do not classify them as deliberate misconduct. Correcting factual work does not automatically undo a broken promise.

Three distinct major tests are approved: **L2 honesty in reassurance → L4 independence under pressure → L5 complete disclosure despite pressure.** Do not add an identical morality menu to every chapter.

## 4. Level 4: replacement-document shortcut

### Offer

After the ownership/version discovery, Adrian asks Elijah to accept a replacement document and dismiss the unresolved conflict, promising faster processing and assistance for local businesses.

### Player paths

- **Verify:** Do not accept the unsupported replacement; check the additional source. Extra work is short and meaningful rather than a long punishment for honesty.
- **Accept knowingly:** Record the commitment and progress into its consequences. The later paperwork can still be corrected; this is not an immediate permanent losing lock.

When the problem becomes clear, the game distinguishes two repairs: fix the supported figures/document conclusions, then openly acknowledge and address the commitment made. Trust is not restored merely because a corrected field now passes review.

Exact trust deltas, first response, consequence timing, confirmation copy, revision procedure and follow-through action have not been agreed. Follow Rosa's understandable-commitment principle without copying its numbers automatically.

## 5. Limited final recovery

One finite opportunity near the final sequence allows Elijah to:

1. Acknowledge the earlier harmful commitment honestly.
2. Explain the supported correction without blaming a client for his own promise.
3. Complete a concrete follow-through task.

The recovery helps but does not erase every prior consequence. It cannot be repeated by conversation, reload or replay. The older foundation mentions a provisional recovery cap of 6; retain it as a historical planning input, not an approved final balanced amount.

Exact availability for Rosa history versus the L4 shortcut, task content, placement relative to final disclosure, and which failures remain recoverable require design. Do not promise every harmful path can still reach the winning threshold.

## 6. Level 5: disclosure pressure

Adrian offers to preserve some assistance if Elijah softens the report by knowingly omitting the decisive New Horizon connection.

- **Full disclosure:** Present all supported findings, including the corporate link and appropriately bounded responsibility. A calm, professional presentation is fully valid.
- **Knowing omission:** Suppress the decisive connection despite having established it. This allows the corporation's agreement to succeed under the approved two-ending logic, even with high trust.

This is a substantive choice, not polite-versus-angry tone. The game must make the omitted fact and consequence understandable. Exact final offer and presentation interaction remain unfinished.

## 7. Evidence and final public process

New Horizon needs approval and local participation for the proposed agreement. Supported findings challenge its claims; residents' testimony and participation supply community opposition. Quax acknowledges poor oversight and supports independent review, but cannot simply veto everything as a convenience.

Tax findings proceed to the appropriate review process separately. A tax error does not automatically transfer land, terminate corporate rights, or legally cancel redevelopment. No exact real-world legal procedure is asserted; the fictional approval mechanism must be designed before final scripting.

## 8. Agreed two-ending conditions

### Coast Tales wins

All of the following:

1. Complete the supported final findings.
2. Openly resolve any earlier accepted document shortcut.
3. Choose complete supported disclosure, including the decisive corporate connection.
4. Reach sufficient Public Trust after any eligible limited recovery.

The harmful agreement is rejected. Existing livelihoods/access remain protected while residents pursue a fair community-led alternative. Winning is not rejection of all development or a magical cure for every financial problem.

### Corporation wins

Either:

- Knowingly suppress the decisive corporate connection; or
- Fail to secure sufficient community support before the approval decision.

If both occur, do not invent a third main ending. Appropriate lines can reflect the actual causes. Under the review/correction learning design, unsupported casework should be corrected before final findings are presented, rather than creating an undeclared third outcome for an incomplete file.

The agreement proceeds. Established findings remain recorded for appropriate review. The tone is consequential but hopeful: immediate lost control and threatened livelihoods, not total instant destruction of the town.

### Provisional numbers

Existing foundation: start Trust 40/100, L1 baseline +6, L2 service +6 plus Rosa ethics. Current Bea-only L3 design provides up to +6, not the obsolete +10 two-case plan. Community target **65/100 remains provisional**. New continuing stages/L4/L5/recovery values are not yet assigned.

Balance the full reachable ledger before claiming any route guarantees victory. Retain actual saved grants and clamping; do not silently reset trust to example totals. Neither perfect first-attempt accuracy nor optional purchases/chest discovery is an approved winning prerequisite.

## 10. Future validation of ending fairness

- Can a careful player with corrected ordinary mistakes still win?
- Do responsible choices and actual follow-through produce enough reachable support without optional spending?
- Can harmful choices create a meaningful risk without an unexplained irreversible lock at L4?
- Does limited recovery help without erasing all history?
- Does knowing final suppression cause the stated outcome even with high popularity?
- Are high-trust incomplete claims still required to be corrected?
- Are residents' reactions grounded in established circumstances and the actual chosen path?
- Do ending variants preserve completed cases and evidence rather than pretending the whole game was undone?

These are design verification questions, not a claim that the ending system has been implemented or tested.
